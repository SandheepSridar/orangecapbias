How the knockouts work — straight from the rule book

Summarised from the NJSBCL Rules and Playing Conditions (2026), sections 6.2 and 7. Worth a read before a knockout game — the tie rules are not the league ones.

Qualification (rule 7)

The top 8 teams from each group qualify — "even if the 9th and lower ranked teams in each group have higher points than the 8th team in the other group." Ranking is on total points, with penalty and bonus points already folded in.

Seeding ties (rule 7.1)

Teams level on points are separated in this order:

  1. Head-to-head — but only if one team beat every other team tied with it. If the results between them form a loop, head-to-head is skipped entirely.
  2. Net run rate, to five decimal places (the site displays three).
  3. NRR against common opponents, ignoring any opponent a tied team was washed out against.

The four rounds (rule 6.2)

Pre-quarter finals → Quarter finals → Semifinals → Final. A knockout match cannot be drawn: level scores go to a Super Over, unlike the group stage where a tie is simply 2 points each.

Super Over (rule 6.2)

Each side nominates three batsmen and one bowler and bats one over; the innings ends early on two wickets. Still level after that:

  1. Most sixes across the main innings plus the super over
  2. Then most fours (a five from an overthrow counts as a boundary)
  3. Then most twos — threes count here too
  4. Then another super over, under the same rules

Umpires run it, and their decisions are final — no escalation to the EC.
